How the model works

Project baseline
Simulation method and its simplifications

Each run samples whether every risk occurs (a Bernoulli draw at its probability) and, if it does, its cost and delay from a triangular distribution (minimum, most likely, maximum). Cost and delay of one event move together: a big event is big on both. Risks are independent of one another. Delays add up, as if every risk sat on the critical path. Every week beyond the 22-week plan adds site overheads. Mitigation spend is certain and comes out of the same budget. Each risk uses a fixed random stream, so the same choices always give the same answer and differences between scenarios come from your choices, not from noise.

Challenge these simplifications in your recommendation. Which risks are likely to be correlated? Which are not on the critical path? What would including them do to your confidence?

Heat forecast

Hot days are counted from daily maximum temperatures, 1 May–30 September each year since 1979, using valid observations only. Years with fewer than 120 valid days are left out. Trend: an ordinary least-squares line through the annual counts, projected to 2027, with each year's residual added back to describe year-to-year variability. Last 10 summers and All summers resample the observed counts. The data refresh weekly from the open-data pipeline in this site's repository. Each hot day that falls during roof works costs £k in standby and loses week once the allowance is used up. Round 2 applies a hotter-than-normal seasonal outlook (×).
